# SEDAR Command Center
## One connected view of the business—from inquiry to safe, profitable service

**Client presentation · MVP system visualization**

> Presenter message: This is not a promise that every external connection is already live. It is a working visualization of how SEDAR's people, processes, records, and management decisions will come together in one system.

---

# The decision this presentation supports

Today, important information can live in separate spreadsheets, messages, folders, and department records.

The proposed SEDAR system brings those activities into one shared operating picture:

**One record entered once → shared with the right departments → turned into action → visible to management.**

The result is faster coordination, stronger controls, fewer surprises, and a clearer view of profitability.

---

# What the MVP is—and is not

## What it is now

- A working business-process model using realistic SEDAR scenarios
- A visual dashboard showing how departments connect
- A way for SEDAR leaders and users to validate screens, responsibilities, approvals, and reports
- A foundation that reduces uncertainty before full deployment

## What it is not yet

- The final production system
- Connected to every live bank, AIS/GPS device, scanner, payroll rule, or Microsoft service
- Loaded with final company records, approval limits, and user permissions

**The purpose of the MVP is to let SEDAR see, test, and shape the right system before committing to full rollout.**

# The core business journey

**Customer need** → **Job order** → **Readiness check** → **Dispatch & schedule** → **Voyage and fuel records** → **Completion** → **Billing & collection** → **Profitability**

The same job record connects commercial terms, vessel assignment, crew readiness, operational activity, fuel and supplier costs, invoice status, and margin.

## Business value

- Less duplicate encoding between departments
- No dispatch without visible readiness checks
- Faster billing after completion
- Profit shown by job, customer, service type, and vessel
- A traceable history from request to collection

---

# Flow 1 — From customer request to dispatch

1. Operations records the customer, service, location, requested schedule, and agreed commercial basis.
2. The dispatcher selects a tug and checks for schedule conflicts.
3. The system checks whether the vessel is blocked by maintenance, missing documents, or another active assignment.
4. Crew assignment is checked for overlap, fit-for-duty medical status, and required verified certificates.
5. The job moves from **Requested** to **Ready** to **Dispatched** only when the required conditions are met.
6. Management sees today's assignments, delays, and reasons in one dispatch view.

**Control gained:** readiness becomes a documented decision, not a last-minute assumption.

---

# Flow 2 — From voyage to billing and profit

1. The crew or operations team records departure, arrival, distance, and voyage details.
2. Fuel entries are tied to the vessel, voyage, and job; quantity and cost are visible.
3. Any vendor bill, spare part usage, maintenance cost, and approved crew cost can be linked to the same job.
4. Operations confirms completion and the agreed tariff produces the billing record.
5. Finance reviews and posts the customer invoice; collection is matched to the correct invoice and bank.
6. The dashboard compares revenue with fuel, supplier, crew, and related costs.

**Management result:** SEDAR can see not only how many jobs were completed, but which work was truly profitable.

---

# Flow 3 — Vessel readiness and maintenance

**Plan** → **Due alert** → **Work order** → **Parts and labor** → **Completion history** → **Next due date**

- Reusable maintenance plans trigger work by date or running hours.
- Defects become work orders with severity, owner, downtime, and cost.
- Critical work can block dispatch until the vessel is ready.
- Required parts show available stock; shortages can begin a purchase request.
- Parts issued to the vessel and labor cost remain connected to the work order.
- Completion creates a permanent equipment-history snapshot and advances the next due date.
- Dry-dock windows, downtime, and cost are visible; full contractor and milestone management is a production-phase enhancement.

**Value:** planned intervention replaces avoidable breakdown and gives management a true cost history per vessel and equipment item.

---

# Flow 4 — Safety, security, and environmental control

**Report** → **Assess** → **Assign** → **Correct** → **Verify** → **Close**

- Incidents and near misses capture what happened, where, when, and who owns the response.
- Risk assessments score hazards and record controls and review status.
- Inspections and audit-type checks record findings and closure evidence.
- Permits show validity, renewal status, approvals, and attachments.
- Training records show completion, certificates, and expiry.
- Open and overdue corrective actions rise to management attention.

**Control gained:** an issue cannot quietly disappear in an email chain; ownership and closure are visible.

---

# Flow 5 — Qualified crew on every assignment

1. HR/Crewing maintains one profile for each crew member.
2. Certifications, STCW records, medical status, training, leave, and rotation are visible together.
3. Expiring documents appear before they become an operational problem.
4. Rotation rules prevent overlapping assignments.
5. Dispatch checks verified documents and fit-for-duty status.
6. Approved crew hours and job-cost allocations prepare the bridge to payroll and job profitability.

**Value:** safer assignments, fewer compliance surprises, better rotation planning, and a clear path from service hours to payroll cost.

---

# Flow 6 — Purchase request to vessel-ready stock

**Need identified** → **Purchase request** → **Budget and approval** → **Supplier comparison** → **Purchase order** → **Receipt** → **Issue to vessel/work order** → **Supplier bill**

- A maintenance shortage or department need starts a purchase request.
- The request shows budget position and urgency.
- Approvers see the reason, vessel/work-order link, supplier options, and history.
- Only accredited suppliers move forward; quotations can be compared.
- An approved request becomes an order, then a warehouse receipt.
- Stock moves record the destination, including vessel locations.
- Finance can trace a disbursement back to the approved order and posted supplier bill.

**Control gained:** purchasing decisions, stock movement, and payment share one evidence trail.

---

# Flow 7 — Inventory and fuel control

- Spare parts, fuel, lubricants, and office supplies use a shared item register.
- Warehouses and locations show where stock is held.
- Receipts, transfers, vessel issues, and work-order consumption create a movement history.
- Reorder points and shortage alerts tell Procurement what needs attention.
- Operational fuel logs connect consumption to the vessel and job.
- Product barcodes are supported; the scanner workflow is configured during production rollout.

**Value:** fewer emergency purchases, less uncertainty about stock, and clearer consumption by vessel and activity.

---

# Flow 8 — Recruitment from public website to employee

This journey crosses the new SEDAR website and several internal business areas.

1. **Public careers page:** SEDAR presents its employer brand and open positions.
2. **Application:** a candidate selects a role, submits identity and contact details, and uploads required documents.
3. **Applicant workspace:** the candidate can review the application/profile and complete missing information.
4. **Recruitment dashboard:** HR sees the pipeline, documents, role, status, and next action.
5. **Screening and interview:** HR dispatches the application for review, schedules interviews, records assessment, and moves the candidate through agreed stages.
6. **Hiring decision:** the approved applicant becomes an employee record instead of being re-encoded.
7. **Onboarding:** identity, employment documents, attendance, performance cycle, and—when applicable—crew qualifications become part of the employee's controlled record.

**Value:** a professional candidate experience outside, and one traceable hiring process inside.

---

# Flow 9 — Employee lifecycle

**Hire** → **Employee record** → **Attendance** → **Performance** → **Development and retention**

- Employee profiles provide the official people record.
- Attendance supports office, standby, and vessel shifts, including overtime review.
- Performance cycles connect goals, employee input, manager scoring, comments, and HR approval.
- Training and compliance records support development and readiness.
- Employee documents have owners, status, and expiry visibility.

Full Philippine payroll computation—taxes, deductions, payslips, payment, and accounting posting—is finalized in the production phase after policy and payroll-rule validation.

---

# Flow 10 — Document and corporate governance

- Contracts, vessel certificates, insurance, permits, board resolutions, and ISO documents have responsible owners and attachments.
- Expiry and renewal views help teams act before a document becomes a business risk.
- Approval status and supporting evidence create accountability.
- Corporate records cover legal matters, internal-audit actions, and board governance.
- Management can see overdue renewals and actions without searching through folders.

**Value:** stronger audit readiness, fewer missed expiries, and clearer corporate control.

# Finance turns activity into management truth

Operational records feed the financial view:

- Customer invoices and collections support Accounts Receivable.
- Supplier bills and controlled disbursements support Accounts Payable.
- Journal reports provide the General Ledger and Trial Balance.
- Cash views show bank position, outstanding checks, and forecasts.
- Budgets compare planned, committed, and actual spending.
- Fixed assets hold vessel/equipment values and depreciation schedules.
- Job profitability combines posted revenue and linked operating costs.

**Management result:** Finance does not wait for separate departmental spreadsheets before explaining performance.

# How the recommended tools fit together

## Recommended core

**Odoo** becomes the shared operating backbone for accounting, purchasing, inventory, people, maintenance, documents, customer activity, and SEDAR-specific tug workflows.

## Added after agreement

- **Power BI:** Board and President dashboards, trends, forecasts, and scheduled management packs
- **Microsoft 365 / Teams:** communication, meetings, and familiar office collaboration
- **SharePoint:** governed document libraries, controlled access, and collaboration
- **DocuSign:** formal electronic signatures for approved contracts and documents
- **AIS/GPS provider:** live vessel position and movement updates inside the operating view
- **Barcode devices:** faster receiving, counting, transfer, and vessel issuance
- **Bank/payroll connections:** implemented after providers, rules, and approval controls are confirmed

Specialized maritime platforms such as ShipNet or ABS NS5 remain future options if fleet scale or technical complexity outgrows the core solution; integration is preferable to rebuilding everything.

# Work timeline

This is the proposed delivery path from the first requirements discussion to a supported handoff.

| Phase | Duration | Status | What happens |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Phase 1: Initial Requirements and Context Gathering | 1 day | Done | Received the requirement list and completed the initial interview to understand SEDAR's operations, departments, and priorities. |
| Phase 2: MVP Development | 1 week | Current stage | Created the MVP based on the requirements and data gathered from Phase 1. This is the dashboard SEDAR can now review, question, and improve. |
| Phase 3: Continuous Iteration | 3-4 months | Next | Weekly visits and testing with the respective departments to collect critiques, confirm real workflows, and improve the system with users. |
| Phase 4: System Finalization | 1 week | Later | Finalize the system and perform department-level testing, with the expectation that only minimal errors or improvements remain. |
| Phase 5: Handoff | 1-3 months | Support period | Formally hand off the system to the company, including free training and free 1-3 months maintenance. |

**Why this matters:** the MVP is not the finish line. It is the decision tool that lets SEDAR see the proposed system early, then shape it department by department before final handoff.

# Appendix — presentation guidance

- Lead with the business journey, not the software menus.
